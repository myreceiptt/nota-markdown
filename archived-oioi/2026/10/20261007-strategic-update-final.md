## 1. Why I Am Sharing This Update

Before tomorrow's meeting, I want to give a short update on where I think the work stands today.

When we started, much of the discussion was about Web3 Login, ALPHA, token flow, and eventually a BGC × iBLOOMING coin.

Over the past year, I have worked through the existing business rules, historical data, simulation design, UI/UX discussions, and technical architecture.

That process has helped me understand something important.

We have several things that can work together, but they do not necessarily belong in the same implementation or need to be completed at the same time.

I now find it useful to separate the work into four areas:

1. **Product direction** — What customers need, what iBLOOMING delivers, and what makes the products valuable.
2. **Web3 infrastructure** — How users log in, establish identity, and interact with wallets or Smart Accounts.
3. **ALPHA product utility** — What users may earn, acquire, spend, or use within the product experience.
4. **Future coin and tokenomics** — Whether a separate coin is justified and how its economics should work.

They can still connect.

I just don't think we should force them into one system before we know what each part is actually for.

This is not a statement that we should stop exploring a coin. It is a recommendation about how we should approach the work without creating unnecessary complexity or committing to decisions too early.

## 2. Where the Work Stands

### 2.1 Product-Led Direction

**Current position: Working strategic direction**

My view at this point is simple: start from the product and the user.

Web3, ALPHA, NFT or other digital credentials, AI, and similar technology should help people do something useful.

Depending on the product, that could mean making a purchase easier, improving access, recording ownership, giving users a reason to return, or creating a better overall experience.

The technology should support that value, not become the reason a product exists.

For iBLOOMING, I think we should examine product opportunities through measurable outcomes.

| Objective | What We Need to Understand |
|---|---|
| Purchase | Does the feature help users decide to buy a product? |
| Usage | Does it encourage users to actually use what they have purchased? |
| Access | Does it make products, features, or services easier to access? |
| Ownership | Is there a meaningful need for a digital entitlement, credential, or proof? |
| Retention | Does it give users a good reason to return? |
| Repeat Purchase | Does the experience encourage customers to buy again? |
| Revenue | Can the product create sustainable business value? |

This is what I mean by **Product-Led**.

It does not mean every feature must produce immediate revenue, or that every blockchain-related idea should be rejected.

It means that we should first understand what value the product delivers and what behaviour we want to improve.

Once that is clear, we can ask whether Web3, ALPHA, AI, or another technology provides a meaningful advantage.

#### Why This Direction Matters

The earlier stages of our work naturally focused on how existing BGC and iBLOOMING mechanisms might be translated into blockchain-based systems.

That was a useful exercise. It required us to understand how the existing business works, how rewards are calculated, how data moves, and where the operational dependencies exist.

But understanding an existing mechanism does not automatically mean that every part of it should become a token or an on-chain transaction.

I would rather identify the product needs first, then choose the technology that helps us solve them.

**My recommendation is to build technology around validated product value, rather than design products merely to give a token something to do.**

This remains a strategic recommendation and should not be confused with a formal founder resolution approving every aspect of a Product-Led transition.

### 2.2 Web3 Login + Smart Account

**Current position: Infrastructure track**

The Web3 Login technical architecture has already been prepared and handed to Yuku's engineering side for implementation.

Its immediate purpose is to establish an identity and wallet-ready foundation for BGC × iBLOOMING users.

The intended user journey discussed with KK and the UI/UX team is:

**BGC × iBLOOMING Account**

→ Sign Up / Login  
→ Activate Smart Account  
→ Accept Smart Account Terms  
→ Personal Wallet / EOA  
→ Smart Account

This describes the proposed account and wallet experience. It does not mean every function had already been deployed.

The important point is that the user should be able to understand and use the account system without first having to become familiar with blockchain terminology or cryptocurrency transactions.

#### What This Infrastructure Is Intended to Provide

The immediate work concerns user identity, authentication, account activation, consent, wallet association, and the technical foundation for future Web3-enabled features.

It does not require us to finish every possible ALPHA function first.

A Smart Account may eventually support product access, utility transactions, digital credentials, or other functions. But that does not mean those functions are automatically included in the initial Web3 Login implementation.

I also want to maintain one important UI/UX distinction.

**Showing a Smart Account does not mean we are asking users to trade cryptocurrency.**

Similarly, the current interface does not need to display ALPHA earning and spending features before those features have been properly defined.

#### The Engineering Dependency

The Web3 Login architecture can progress as a separate infrastructure workstream.

The engineering team should not have to wait for complete ALPHA tokenomics merely to implement identity, login, account activation, or wallet-ready infrastructure.

At the same time, I understand the concern raised during the UI/UX discussions: users should have a clear reason to activate a Smart Account.

We should address that concern by identifying useful product features while the infrastructure is being developed.

We do not need to invent final ALPHA economics simply to justify a wallet interface.

### 2.3 ALPHA Product Utility

**Current position: Product-utility layer under definition**

My suggestion is that ALPHA should first be tested as an internal Product-Led utility.

In practical terms, this means ALPHA should have a clear purpose in the product experience.

Users should be able to understand why they might need it, how they obtain it, and what they can do with it.

I see four possible utility areas worth examining.

#### Earn

Which eligible activities should allow users to earn ALPHA?

An activity should have a genuine connection to a product objective. We should understand why we want to reward that behaviour and whether the reward improves the experience or business outcome.

#### Acquire

Should users be able to acquire or top up ALPHA through an approved mechanism?

This may include fiat payments, as discussed with KK, but the mechanism itself still requires product, economic, accounting, and operational decisions.

#### Spend

Which iBLOOMING products, reports, content, features, or services should accept ALPHA?

This is particularly important because spending creates a direct relationship between the utility and the products available in the ecosystem.

#### Access / Credentials

Could ALPHA or a separate digital credential provide access rights, verifiable participation, ownership records, or other meaningful benefits?

Not every access or ownership feature needs a fungible token. We should choose the appropriate mechanism for each product need.

#### What Has Not Been Finalised

Although these possible utilities have been discussed, we have not yet fixed the complete earning, acquisition, spending, conversion, issuance, and economic rules.

Before those rules are finalised, we need to know:

- Which existing products or activities are suitable.
- Whether new product activities are needed.
- What behaviour each utility is intended to improve.
- Whether fiat acquisition is appropriate and approved.
- How ALPHA is valued, issued, used, or consumed.
- What accounting and operational responsibilities arise.
- How the system will measure whether the utility is useful.

The intention is to define a product utility that makes sense before committing to its detailed implementation.

#### Relationship with Earlier ALPHA Work

Earlier documents also considered ALPHA in relation to the existing BGC business rules and simulation.

That work remains part of our project history.

However, I do not want to assume that existing Purchase Credits, Sales Points, affiliate rewards, pools, or obligations must automatically be converted into ALPHA balances.

Those mechanisms already have their own business definitions and responsibilities.

Any connection to ALPHA should be justified, specified, and tested rather than treated as an automatic consequence of tokenisation.

### 2.4 SIMALPHA

**Current position: Internal decision-support tool**

The simulator is useful because it allows us to examine assumptions and compare possible scenarios.

It helps us understand how different choices might affect outcomes, especially when economic parameters, user behaviour, and operational costs change.

I see SIMALPHA as a way to support better decisions, not as a tool that automatically supplies the correct answer.

For example, simulation can help us examine:

- How sensitive a result is to a particular assumption.
- What changes under different demand or usage scenarios.
- Which variables have the greatest effect on costs or liabilities.
- Where available data supports a conclusion.
- Where the model still depends on assumptions or incomplete coverage.

This distinction is important.

A simulator can produce precise-looking numbers even when some of its assumptions remain uncertain.

**The precision of an output does not necessarily establish the validity of the underlying economic model.**

I would not present our simulation work as proof that final tokenomics are ready, that all business obligations have been validated, or that a future public coin can safely proceed.

The simulation should help us identify what needs to be tested or decided next.

It should not be used to create confidence that the available evidence does not yet support.

### 2.5 Future BGC × iBLOOMING Coin

**Current position: Separate future decision**

For the future coin, I don't think we should lock the model yet.

Before deciding supply, distribution, pricing, conversion mechanisms, or a market strategy, we need to understand what role the coin would actually play in the business.

Names such as **iBC** and **iBTC** have been discussed. Another model may also be proposed by the founders.

The name itself is not the important decision.

I would first want to understand:

- Why the business needs a separate coin.
- Why users or market participants would want it.
- What creates genuine demand.
- How value enters and leaves the system.
- How the coin relates to actual products and services.
- What obligations or risks the mechanism creates.
- How the proposed economics could remain sustainable.
- Which legal, operational, accounting, and market questions must be addressed.

The Product-Led ALPHA approach can help us generate evidence.

If ALPHA utility later proves to improve product usage, purchases, retention, or revenue, those findings may inform the design of future coin economics.

But that progression is not automatic.

A useful internal ALPHA system does not necessarily mean we must launch a public token.

The future coin should remain a separate decision, with its own business case and validation requirements.

## 3. How I Think the Layers Should Connect

The layers should connect, but they should not be confused.

We should begin with a real product or user need.

Then we define what we want to improve.

Only after that do we decide how Web3 infrastructure and ALPHA utility might help.

### 3.1 Product / User Need

What does the user actually need, buy, use, return to, or value?

This is where I want every new proposal to begin.

We should be able to explain the customer's problem, expectation, or motivation without referring to a coin.

### 3.2 Product-Led Objective

Once the need is understood, we define the intended outcome.

That could be:

**Purchase · Usage · Access · Retention · Revenue**

We may also examine repeat behaviour, ownership, completion, or other measurable results.

The objective should guide the technology decisions that follow.

### 3.3 Two Parallel Workstreams

I suggest that we proceed with two separate but coordinated tracks.

**Workstream A — Web3 Login + Smart Account**

This provides the identity, account, authentication, activation, and wallet-ready infrastructure.

The implementation can continue within the agreed infrastructure scope.

**Workstream B — ALPHA Product Utility**

This defines the eligible products and activities, intended user benefits, possible earning and acquisition methods, spending cases, and the rules needed for each.

The two workstreams can move in parallel.

We do not need to wait for all ALPHA economics before progressing with identity infrastructure. Likewise, completing Web3 Login does not automatically complete the ALPHA product model.

### 3.4 Product Experience

Once the relevant infrastructure and utility are ready, they should support real interactions within the product.

Examples might include access to a purchased report, use of a digital service, participation in an eligible activity, or verification of an entitlement.

The implementation must be connected to an actual user experience.

### 3.5 Evidence

After implementation or testing, we should examine what users actually do.

Are they purchasing? Returning? Using the product? Completing activities? Finding lasting value?

Evidence should help us distinguish useful functionality from functionality that merely exists.

### 3.6 Future Coin Decision

Only after understanding the relevant product behaviour and economic implications should we decide whether a future coin is justified.

The conceptual sequence is:

**Product / User Need**

↓

**Product-Led Objective**

↓

**Two Parallel Tracks**

- Web3 Login + Smart Account
- ALPHA Product Utility

↓

**Product Experience**

↓

**Evidence: Conversion · Usage · Retention · Revenue · Repeat Behaviour**

↓

**Future Coin / Tokenomics Decision**

This is a decision-making framework, not a statement that every component had already been implemented.

### 3.7 Keeping the Responsibilities Separate

| Layer | Its Main Purpose | What It Does Not Automatically Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Web3 Login | Identity, login, and wallet-ready infrastructure | Users must trade crypto |
| Smart Account | Account infrastructure for future features | ALPHA economics are complete |
| ALPHA | Candidate internal product utility | Public coin or market listing |
| Future Coin | Separate economic and possibly market-facing model | Necessary simply because Web3 is being used |

**Web3 Login is not ALPHA, and ALPHA is not automatically the future coin.**

The separation is intentional.

It allows Engineering to move forward while we continue defining product utility and validating economics before making larger decisions.

## 4. One ALPHA Use Case We Already Discussed

A useful example came from the discussion with KK.

He raised the possibility that users might eventually use ALPHA to purchase iBLOOMING products.

In that scenario, users could acquire or top up ALPHA using fiat, then use ALPHA through their Smart Account to purchase selected reports, products, or services.

The possible flow is:

**FIAT / CASH**

↓

**ACQUIRE / TOP UP ALPHA**

↓

**SMART ACCOUNT**

↓

**USE ALPHA**

↓

**SELECTED iBLOOMING PRODUCTS / REPORTS / SERVICES**

This is a possible product flow, **not a final economic rule**.

### 4.1 Why I Think This Is Worth Exploring

This creates a practical connection between ALPHA and actual products.

Instead of asking users to acquire a token without knowing what it is for, the system could potentially connect acquisition and spending to products users already want.

That gives us a clearer way to discuss utility.

We could examine whether the mechanism improves product access, purchase behaviour, or the overall experience.

However, we should not assume it provides an advantage merely because the flow is technically possible.

### 4.2 What Needs to Be Decided First

Before implementing this use case, I think the founders and Product Team should determine the following.

**Eligible products**

Which reports, services, features, or other products can accept ALPHA?

**Acquisition mechanism**

Will users be permitted to acquire or top up ALPHA using fiat?

If yes, how should that process work?

**Economic definition**

What does one unit of ALPHA represent? How does it relate to a product price, an internal right, or another defined unit?

**Earning rules**

Can users earn ALPHA through eligible product activities as well as acquire it?

**Spending and conversion rules**

What happens when ALPHA is spent? How is the purchase recorded and fulfilled? What happens to the ALPHA used?

**Issuance and accounting**

How are balances created, recorded, reconciled, and accounted for?

**User experience**

What should the user see and understand about balances, purchases, terms, and account activation?

**Economic validation**

What are the effects on business revenue, operational costs, liabilities, and overall sustainability?

These are open decisions. They should not be mistaken for mechanisms already approved or implemented.

### 4.3 Why This Belongs Outside the Initial Web3 Login Scope

When KK raised this possible fiat-to-ALPHA purchase flow, my response was that it should be included in the ALPHA product-utility discussion.

It should not be treated as part of the immediate Web3 Login implementation by default.

KK agreed with that distinction.

That alignment was useful because it allowed us to preserve the current infrastructure scope while recognising a possible future product feature.

It was not a final approval of the fiat top-up model or all its economic rules.

### 4.4 A Practical Point About Smart Account Activation

A concern raised in the UI/UX discussion was that users should have a clear reason to activate a Smart Account.

I think that concern is valid.

The answer is not to stop all infrastructure work until complete tokenomics are available.

Nor is it to invent token features prematurely.

The better approach is to continue the Web3 Login infrastructure while defining ALPHA's first meaningful product use cases.

That way, Engineering can make progress, and the Product Team can establish a clear reason for users to benefit from the account.

## 5. A Framework for Evaluating the Next iBLOOMING Business Idea

I want to hear the new iBLOOMING idea first.

Then I want to map it against what we have already learned instead of forcing an answer before understanding the proposal.

New ideas are welcome.

The purpose of this framework is not to restrict them, but to understand **what should be built, why it matters, and in what sequence**.

The evaluation sequence is:

**User Need → Product / Service → Business Model → Desired Behaviour → Utility → Technology → Economics & Risk → Evidence**

### 5.1 User Need

**What real problem or desire are we solving?**

Who is the intended user?

Why would that person care about this product or service?

What is missing from the current experience?

I want to understand the need before discussing the technology.

### 5.2 Product & Business

**What are users actually buying, using, or accessing?**

What does the business deliver?

What makes the offering valuable?

How does the business earn revenue?

One question I would keep in the discussion is:

> **Would it still create customer value without an income opportunity or a coin?**

I think this is an important test.

It is not meant to dismiss the existing affiliate business or oppose the possibility of a token.

It helps us examine whether the proposed product delivers value in its own right.

If the product is genuinely useful, we have a stronger foundation for designing additional incentives or utility mechanisms.

### 5.3 Desired Behaviour

**What do we want to improve?**

Depending on the idea, the objective might be:

- Purchase conversion.
- Product usage.
- Completion of an activity.
- Retention.
- Repeat purchase.
- Referral.
- Access.
- Ownership.

Before creating an earning or reward mechanism, I want to know why we want users to perform the activity.

Otherwise, we may create activity without establishing whether it benefits the product or the business.

### 5.4 Utility & Technology

**What should ALPHA, Web3, AI, or digital credentials actually do?**

Does the idea need a blockchain-based entitlement, a Smart Account, a digital record, an automated service, a payment mechanism, or a verifiable credential?

Could ordinary technology solve the same problem more clearly and with less complexity?

If a simpler solution works better, I would not force blockchain into it.

Technology selection should follow the product requirement.

### 5.5 Coin & Economics

**Why does the idea need a coin?**

What creates demand for it?

How does value move through the business?

How are users expected to benefit?

Does the coin create additional responsibilities, costs, or liabilities?

If a public market is involved, what happens when market behaviour differs from the behaviour of actual product users?

I would rather understand those questions before choosing a supply figure, conversion formula, or market strategy.

The important thing is not whether we can issue a coin.

It is whether the proposed economic mechanism supports the business and its users in a sustainable way.

### 5.6 Evidence

**What must we measure before scaling?**

The important areas include:

**Usage · Conversion · Retention · Repeat Behaviour · Revenue · Cost · Liabilities · Sustainability**

We should decide which assumptions can be tested through historical data, which need simulation, and which require actual product usage.

I do not expect every answer to exist on the first day.

But a useful proposal should help us identify what evidence we need and what findings would cause us to adjust the plan.

## 6. For the 8 October Founder Meeting

My immediate objective is to **listen first**.

KK has informed me that the founders want to discuss a new iBLOOMING business idea that may also connect to “our coin.”

At this point, I do not want to assume what that relationship means.

It may involve ALPHA, a future coin such as iBC or iBTC, or a different model being considered by the founders.

We need to hear the proposal before determining which interpretation is appropriate.

### 6.1 What I Want to Understand

During the meeting, I want to understand the new idea as completely as possible.

In particular:

1. What business or product activity is being proposed?
2. Who are the intended users or customers?
3. What problem does the idea solve?
4. What value will the users receive?
5. How will the business earn revenue?
6. Why is a coin relevant to the proposed model?
7. What role will ALPHA, Web3, AI, or Smart Accounts play?
8. Will the new idea affect existing BGC or iBLOOMING mechanisms?
9. What would the founders like to achieve first?

I do not need to answer every question immediately.

I would rather understand the idea properly than give advice based on incomplete information.

### 6.2 What I Intend to Do After the Meeting

Once the idea is clear, I can evaluate it against the work we have already completed.

That assessment should examine the product, the customer experience, the proposed business model, and the relationship with existing or proposed technology.

It should also identify unresolved economic, operational, and implementation questions.

From there, I can prepare a more structured recommendation for the founders.

The intention is to make the next discussion more productive, with a clearer explanation of:

- What the new idea requires.
- Which existing work can support it.
- Which parts need further clarification.
- What could be tested first.
- Which decisions belong to the founders.
- What the engineering or product teams would need to implement.

This describes my intended approach before the meeting, not a record of what was subsequently decided.

## 8. Important Boundaries of This Update

I want this document to remain useful when we refer to it later.

For that reason, I think it is important to be clear about what the update establishes and what it does not.

### What This Update Establishes

As of 7 October 2026, my working assessment is that:

- Product needs should guide technology and utility decisions.
- Web3 Login and Smart Account infrastructure can progress independently of final ALPHA economics.
- ALPHA should be examined through actual product use cases.
- The possible fiat-to-ALPHA purchase flow deserves further product and economic evaluation.
- SIMALPHA can support decisions but does not automatically validate final tokenomics.
- A future coin should be treated as a separate economic decision.
- The founders' new business proposal should be understood before we recommend how it should be implemented.

### What This Update Does Not Establish

This document does not mean that:

- All founders have formally approved every part of a Product-Led strategy.
- Web3 Login and Smart Account implementation has been completed.
- ALPHA earning, spending, acquisition, or conversion rules are final.
- Fiat top-up has been fully approved.
- ALPHA is automatically the same as a future public coin.
- A future coin has been approved for issuance, trading, or listing.
- The simulator has proven complete economic sustainability.
- The founders' new business proposal has already been evaluated.
- A recommendation has already been made about an idea that had not yet been presented.

I want to preserve these distinctions because project documents can easily become misleading when working recommendations are later read as final decisions.

## 9. Closing Perspective

We have spent considerable time trying to understand the existing business, its mechanisms, the possibilities of Web3, and the relationship between products and token economics.

That work should not be lost simply because a new idea arrives.

At the same time, the work should not prevent us from considering something better.

I want to use what we have learned as a foundation for understanding the next proposal.

We can remain open to new business models, new product opportunities, and a future coin while continuing to ask what users actually need and what the business can realistically sustain.

The point is not to make technology more complicated.

It is to make our decisions clearer.

**We are not trying to fit the business around a coin.**

**We are building enough evidence to know where a coin can genuinely strengthen the business.**
